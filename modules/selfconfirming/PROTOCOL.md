# Self-Confirming Allocation under Endogenous Demand — Experiment Protocol

Tier-zero (PRB-level) case study for the CoAdapt CAREER premise. The purpose is
to **prove and photograph** the central failure mode before committing to the
full OpenAirInterface + Isaac Sim testbed, and to be able to **falsify** it if
it does not hold.

## The claim under test

> A utility-maximizing allocator that treats slice demand as an exogenous
> process locks into a self-confirming, degraded operating point when endpoint
> demand is generated by a bistable (threshold + hysteresis) adaptation policy.
> An allocator that treats its own influence on future demand as a control
> lever escapes the trap. The two states are indistinguishable in passive
> telemetry and separable by one bounded, SLA-safe probe.

If this reproduces, it is the load-bearing preliminary result for Thrust 1 and
the motivation for Thrusts 2 and 3. If it does not, the premise is fragile and
we need to know now.

## What is modeled

A single adaptive slice sharing a PRB budget, abstracted at the level that
produced the existing oscillation-index preliminary numbers. No radio or vision
stack is required at this tier.

- **Endpoint** — a hidden bistable response operator. Low mode = reduced
  fidelity, small demand, poor inspection quality. High mode = full-fidelity
  offload, large demand, high quality, reachable only under sustained service.
  Hysteresis and an adaptation delay make the two modes genuinely distinct
  operating points rather than a smooth gain.
- **Service** — deliverable rate `service = theta * capacity * channel`.
- **Allocators** — consume only network-observable, allocation-tagged measured
  demand. Application mode and inspection utility are logged as ground truth for
  validation only, never fed to the controller. This is the identifiability
  discipline the proposal commits to.

Controllers compared: `A_exo` (demand-tracking), `A_endo` (cultivation with a
bounded SLA-safe probe and a sustain latch), and static low / static-high
(oracle) references.

## Experiments, and the reviewer objection each one kills

### Exp 1 — Measured response landscape
Sweep a static allocation up then down, letting the endpoint settle at each
level. The ascending and descending branches separate into a hysteresis loop,
giving two self-consistent operating points.
**Defends:** the degraded equilibrium is real, not a cartoon. Turns Figure 4 of
the proposal from a drawing into measured data.

### Exp 2 — Self-confirming lock-in (the money shot)
From an identical low start, run `A_exo`, `A_endo`, and static-high. The
demand-tracker allocates to serve its measured (suppressed) demand, never
provisions enough sustained service to cross the upgrade threshold, and
converges reporting stable, SLA-satisfied, efficient operation at low utility.
The cultivator probes, reveals the latent high mode, latches, and sustains it.
**Defends:** the failure is intrinsic to demand-tracking, not a tuning artifact.
No allocation margin escapes it, because the anchor itself is suppressed.
**Honest caveat to state in the proposal:** the cultivator reaches high utility
at *higher* average PRB, because the high mode genuinely needs more resource.
The contribution is that the extra resource is *justified by demand the
tracker never discovers*, not that escape is free.

### Exp 3 — Indistinguishability and the discriminating probe
Build two traces with near-identical current measured load: one genuinely low
workload, one high workload suppressed into low mode. A passive classifier on
pre-probe telemetry sits near chance. One bounded probe above the SLA floor
produces a delayed demand rise in the suppressed case and nothing in the
genuinely-low case, separating them cleanly.
**Defends:** active identification recovers what passive telemetry cannot, and
the probe is small and SLA-safe. This is the identifiability crux of R1.1.

### Exp 4 — When the trap actually exists
Sweep endpoint sensitivity, resource ceiling, and adaptation memory; report the
tail utility gap between cultivation and demand-tracking, averaged over noisy
channel realizations.
**Defends:** turns an anecdote into a characterized operating region. The gap
appears only when the endpoint is sufficiently sensitive and there is enough
slack to cultivate, and vanishes otherwise. **Naming when ignoring endogeneity
does not matter is a credibility asset, not a weakness.**

## The predictive villain and the two failure regimes

The proposal's antagonist is a predict-then-allocate controller, not a reactive
tracker, because AI-native RAN forecasts demand and pre-allocates. Rebuilding
the villain that way (`predictive_regimes.py`) produced an honest and useful
result.

- **Single-slice, allocation-only predict-then-allocate locks in. It does not
  oscillate.** When measured demand is low the controller has no reason to push
  up, so it settles into the degraded point. Swept across cadence and gain, the
  oscillation index stays at zero. This is physics, not a tuning miss, and it is
  worth stating plainly.

- **Oscillation lives in the disclosure loop.** The network optimistically
  signals a target rate, the endpoint ramps up on belief, the allocation does
  not back the promise, service falls short, the endpoint drops, and the loop
  limit-cycles. Utility collapses to ~0.17 and rings. When the allocation backs
  the signal, the same loop holds at 1.0. This is the allocation-backed
  credibility thesis of Thrust 2, demonstrated, and it says credibility rather
  than cadence is what stabilizes the loop: no update rate rescues an unbacked
  promise.

So lock-in and oscillation are the two ways predict-then-allocate breaks under
endogenous demand, but they live in two different mechanisms. Lock-in is an
allocation failure; oscillation is a disclosure-credibility failure. Do not
claim a single-slice allocator oscillates, because it does not. Figure:
`figures/fig_regimes.png`.

## Rigor rules

- Replay identical channel traces across every controller comparison so a
  difference is attributable to the controller, not luck.
- Multiple seeds; report intervals on any headline number.
- Keep honest baselines (proportional-fair, SLA-threshold, KPI-only predictor,
  periodic re-identification, static conservative, oracle high) so no result is
  a strawman-versus-hero comparison.
- Network-side estimator uses only network-observable signals; every KPI sample
  is tagged with the allocation that produced it.

## Falsification criteria

The premise is in trouble, and we want to discover it here rather than in
review, if any of the following hold across a reasonable parameter range:

- `A_exo` escapes lock-in without cultivation (demand-tracking is already fine).
- The passive classifier separates the two traces (no genuine identifiability
  problem; the states were never ambiguous).
- Lock-in appears only for a contrived, knife-edge endpoint configuration
  (the failure is not robust enough to build a five-year program on).

Exp 4 is the honesty check. Map the region where the trap lives rather than
hunting for one lucky configuration.

## Two-tier fidelity plan

- **Tier zero (this module).** Parametric PRB-level abstraction. Fast, laptop,
  no GPU. Earns the theorem and the clean figures, and is where the regime is
  tuned until lock-in reproduces reliably.
- **Tier one (InspectCell, later).** Full OpenAirInterface + Isaac Sim closed
  loop. Confirms the abstraction's predictions survive a real slice and a real
  detector. Carries Exp 2, 3, and the drift result. GPU-bound, fewer runs, this
  is where the belief is earned.
